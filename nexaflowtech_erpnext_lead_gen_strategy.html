LinkedIn (Organic + Outbound)

Best for ERP

Organic strategy

  • Post 3x/week: ERP transformation stories, before/after case studies, ROI calculators
  • Use founder's personal profile — not company page (3x reach)
  • Target: "Operations Manager India Manufacturing" hashtags

Outbound (Sales Navigator)

  • Filter: Industry + Company size 50–500 + Title (CFO, Ops Head, IT Manager)
  • Connection request → value message → follow-up after 5 days
  • Send only 20–30 requests/day to avoid restriction

Cold Email

High volume, lower conversion
  • Use Apollo.io or Hunter.io to find verified emails of CFOs/Ops Heads in target industries
  • Send from a warmed domain (not your main domain — protect deliverability)
  • Subject lines: "Your competitors in [city] already migrated from Tally" / "Reducing ₹X inventory loss with ERPNext"
  • Sequence: Day 1 email → Day 4 follow-up → Day 9 breakup email
  • Open rate target: 35%+, Reply rate: 4–8% is good for ERP cold email

Manufacturing — messaging hooks

"Your production team is running 3 WhatsApp groups to track inventory. Your finance team has a different number. Let's fix that — with one system that shows real-time stock, BOM, and job cards on a single screen."
"How much inventory shrinkage did you accept last year as 'normal'? Our Manufacturing ERP clients typically find 8–15% of that was recoverable with better tracking."
"LinkedIn hook: I noticed [Company] has 4 plants across Gujarat. How are you handling multi-location inventory reconciliation today — Excel, Tally, or something custom?"

Key angle: Lead with operational chaos → introduce ERPNext as the unifier → offer free process audit to show the gap.

FMCG — messaging hooks

"Every ₹1 of expired stock that hits your warehouse floor costs you ₹3 — write-off + lost margin + compliance. ERPNext batch tracking eliminates this. Can we show you how in 20 minutes?"
"Your distributors are still calling your sales team for stock updates. There's a portal for that. Want to see it running live for an FMCG company similar to yours?"

Key angle: GST/e-Invoice compliance + distributor visibility + batch/expiry tracking are the three FMCG triggers. Lead with whichever the prospect mentions first.

Real Estate — messaging hooks

"Your site engineers know the actual project cost. Your finance team knows what was budgeted. Do those two numbers ever match? ERPNext bridges that gap — project costing synced with accounts in real-time."
"Most real estate companies we speak to lose 6–12% project margin to unapproved purchases and delayed subcontractor reconciliation. We built an ERPNext module specifically for this. 15-minute demo?"

Key angle: Project cost visibility + CRM-to-booking-to-revenue tracking. Real estate CFOs respond to margin leakage framing.

Irresistible offers to drive demos

  • Free ERP Readiness Audit — 45-min session mapping their current chaos to ERPNext modules; delivers a gap report they keep regardless
  • Industry-configured free demo — not a generic ERPNext demo, but one pre-loaded with their industry's chart of accounts, BOM structure, and workflows
  • Pilot project (paid, small scope) — implement 1 module (e.g., Inventory or HR) in 3 weeks, then expand; reduces their risk
  • Migration guarantee — "We migrate your Tally data or you don't pay for data migration phase" — eliminates the #1 objection
  • ROI calculator — interactive tool showing estimated savings based on their employee count / inventory value

Lead → Demo → Conversion funnel

1

Awareness (top of funnel)

LinkedIn content + cold outreach + Google Ads → prospect lands on industry-specific landing page or sees your post. KPI: 500–1000 impressions/week per vertical. Tool: Buffer (scheduling), LinkedIn Sales Nav

2

Lead capture

Offer lead magnet (ROI calculator / "10 signs you've outgrown Tally" PDF) in exchange for email + phone. Or LinkedIn DM reply triggers CRM entry. Tool: Typeform or native LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms → Zapier → CRM

3

Qualification call (15 min)

Quick BANT check: Budget confirmed? Authority to decide? Need (specific pain)? Timeline (within 6 months?). Disqualify fast — don't waste demo slots on non-buyers. Tool: Calendly for booking, Notion for call notes

4

Industry-specific demo (45 min)

Show ERPNext pre-configured for their industry. Walk through their top 3 pain points. Always end with: "Does this solve what you described?" and "Who else needs to see this before a decision?" Tool: Loom for async follow-up demo recording

5

Proposal + pilot offer

Send within 24 hours of demo. Include: scope, timeline, 3 pricing tiers (Basic / Standard / Enterprise), ROI projection for their specific numbers. Offer the paid pilot as a low-risk entry point. Tool: Proposify or PandaDoc

6

Follow-up sequence (7–21 days)

Day 1: Proposal sent + Loom walkthrough. Day 4: Check-in, share a relevant case study. Day 10: "Did the team get a chance to review?" Day 18: Breakup email with final offer. Tool: HubSpot or Zoho CRM sequences

7

Close + onboarding kickoff

Contract signed → kickoff call within 48 hours (while excitement is high). Start discovery immediately. Never let a signed deal go cold. Tool: Frappe Projects or ClickUp for project tracking, Zoho Sign for contracts

8 critical mistakes — and how NexaFlowTech avoids them

Mistake 1: Generic "we implement ERPNext" positioning

Agencies say "we do ERPNext" — every other agency says the same. No differentiation.

Fix:

Niche down by industry. Be "ERPNext for Manufacturing companies in Gujarat" or "ERPNext for FMCG distributors." Specificity converts 3x better.

Mistake 2: Leading with features in outreach

"ERPNext has 15 modules including HR, CRM, Accounts..." — nobody cares until they feel the pain.

Fix:

Lead with their pain. "How are you handling multi-warehouse reconciliation today?" is more powerful than any feature list.

Mistake 3: Giving demos to unqualified leads

Spending 45 minutes showing ERPNext to someone who has no budget or no authority to decide.

Fix:

Always run a 15-minute qualification call first. BANT qualify: Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline. If two fail, don't demo.

Mistake 4: Sending generic ERPNext demos

Showing the default ERPNext demo instance is underwhelming. Prospects can't visualize their business in it.

Fix:

Pre-configure industry-specific demo environments. A textile demo should have BOM for fabric, a real estate demo should show project cost tracking.

Mistake 5: Slow follow-up after demo

Sending the proposal 5 days after the demo — the prospect's excitement is gone and competitors have moved in.

Fix:

Send proposal within 24 hours, include a Loom recap video of the demo walking through their specific pain points. Follow up on Day 4, Day 10, Day 18.

Mistake 6: Competing on price with low-cost developers

Trying to win by being the cheapest — attracts the worst clients and destroys margins.

Fix:

Compete on outcome, not price. "Our implementation reduces your inventory discrepancy by X%" is worth more than "we're ₹2L cheaper." Show ROI, not rates.

Mistake 7: No case studies or social proof

Agencies with zero published case studies lose deals to agencies with even one well-documented success story.

Fix:

Document every implementation as a case study — even internal ones. Format: Client industry + Problem → ERPNext solution → Measurable outcome (₹ saved, time reduced, errors eliminated).

Mistake 8: No retainer / post-go-live engagement

Closing a one-time implementation and moving on — missing the highest-margin revenue: AMC, support, and customization expansions.

Fix:

Every implementation contract should include an AMC offer (Annual Maintenance Contract) and a 6-month customization retainer. Existing clients cost 5x less to expand than acquiring new ones.
